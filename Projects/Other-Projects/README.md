# 🧰 Other 3D Printing Projects

Part of the [Purpose in Practice 3D Printing Workshop](../../README.md).

Not every 3D printing project needs its own GitHub repository.

Sometimes I need a bracket, an adapter, a replacement part, or something that solves a problem around the house.

Sometimes I just want to see if an idea will work.

This is where those projects live.

Some will be useful. Some will be experiments. And some will probably need three revisions before they do what I originally intended.

That's part of the fun.

---

## 🛠️ What You'll Find Here

This directory is for smaller projects that don't necessarily belong in my larger electronics repositories.

Projects may include:

- Household repairs and replacement parts
- Custom brackets and mounting hardware
- Electronics accessories
- Cable management solutions
- Adapters and spacers
- Workshop organization
- Small functional prints
- Experimental designs
- Remixes and modifications of existing models

The focus is on practical designs, but I'm not opposed to printing something just because it looks interesting.

## 📝 What I'll Document

Whenever practical, individual projects will include:

### The Problem

What was I trying to solve?

### The Design

How did I approach the problem, and what did I create?

### Printing Information

- Printer
- Filament
- Layer height
- Wall loops
- Infill
- Supports
- Print orientation
- Any special settings

### Assembly

If the project requires hardware or additional assembly, I'll document those steps.

### Results

Did it work?

What would I change?

Would I print it again?

## 🔄 Prototypes & Revisions

I don't expect every design to work perfectly the first time.

When a project goes through multiple revisions, I'll try to document what changed and why.

For example:

| Revision | Changes |
|---|---|
| V1 | Initial prototype |
| V2 | Fit and clearance adjustments |
| V3 | Strength or assembly improvements |

These are example revision labels. Actual project histories will reflect the designs I create.

Sometimes the most useful information isn't the finished model.

It's understanding why the first version didn't work.

## 🤝 Remixes & Attribution

I enjoy building on ideas shared by other makers.

When a project is based on someone else's design, I'll identify the original creator, link to the source, and follow the applicable license.

I'll distinguish between:

- Original designs
- Modified or remixed designs
- Unmodified third-party designs referenced for inspiration

Not every publicly available 3D model permits redistribution or commercial use.

I'll respect those restrictions and link to the original files when redistribution isn't permitted.

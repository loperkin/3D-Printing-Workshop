# 🔲 Build Plates

Part of the [Purpose in Practice 3D Printing Workshop](../README.md).

A good build plate can make the difference between a successful print and a pile of plastic spaghetti.

This page documents the build plates I use, why I use them, and what I've learned along the way.

---

## BIQU CryoGrip Pro Glacier

My primary build plate is the **BIQU CryoGrip Pro Glacier** for the Bambu Lab A1.

I started using this plate to improve print adhesion and reliability. It's been a useful addition to my printing setup.

### Why I Use It

Reliable first-layer adhesion is important, especially when printing functional parts that may take several hours to complete.

The CryoGrip Pro Glacier has helped me achieve more consistent adhesion without constantly fighting the first layer.

### My Setup

- **Printer:** [Bambu Lab A1](https://geni.us/2uwo9G)
- **Build Plate:** [BIQU CryoGrip Pro Glacier](https://geni.us/ubKy1)
- **Primary Material:** [Bambu Lab PETG Basic](https://geni.us/4HC3cQ)
- **Additional Material:** [Bambu Lab PETG Translucent](https://geni.us/jEhyD)
- **Slicer:** Bambu Studio

### What I Print on It

I primarily use this plate for:

- Electronics enclosures
- ESPHome project cases
- Brackets and mounting hardware
- Functional PETG parts
- Prototypes and test prints

### Tips & Lessons Learned

- Keep the build surface clean and free of oils from handling.
- Make sure the selected build plate and temperature settings match the surface you're using.
- Allow prints to cool before attempting removal.
- Follow the manufacturer's cleaning and maintenance instructions to avoid damaging the coating.
- Test new filament profiles before committing to a long print.

I'll update this section as I experiment with additional materials and settings.

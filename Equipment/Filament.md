# 🧵 Filament & Materials

Part of the [Purpose in Practice 3D Printing Workshop](../README.md).

There are plenty of filament types available, but most of my projects have one thing in common: **they need to be useful**.

I primarily print with **Bambu Lab PETG**, using both PETG Basic and PETG Translucent.

Whether I'm building an electronics enclosure, designing a bracket, or making something around the house, I generally want a material that's durable, practical, and reasonably easy to print.

---

## Why PETG?

PLA is great for many projects, but PETG has become my preferred material for functional prints.

PETG offers several advantages:

- Better heat resistance than standard PLA
- Good toughness and impact resistance
- Some flexibility before breaking
- Good layer adhesion when printed correctly
- Suitable for many electronics enclosures and household parts

PETG isn't perfect. It can be more prone to stringing, moisture-related printing problems, and surface imperfections than PLA.

But for the projects I typically build, I find its properties useful.

**Important:** PETG is not automatically suitable for high-temperature, outdoor, electrical-safety-critical, or load-bearing applications. Material selection should always consider the intended environment and stresses.

## Bambu Lab PETG Translucent

I also use **Bambu Lab PETG Translucent**.

Translucent filament is useful when a project needs to let some light pass through the printed material.

Potential applications include:

- LED indicators
- Light diffusers
- Electronics enclosures with internal status lights
- Decorative accents
- Projects where I want a different visual appearance

Translucent PETG isn't the same as optically clear plastic. The finished appearance depends on color, wall thickness, layer lines, infill, and print settings.

## Filament Storage & Moisture

One thing worth understanding about PETG is that it can absorb moisture from the air.

Wet filament can contribute to:

- Stringing
- Popping or crackling during extrusion
- Rough surface finishes
- Inconsistent extrusion
- Reduced print quality

I recommend storing filament in a dry environment and drying it when necessary, following the filament manufacturer's recommendations.

I'll document my filament storage and drying equipment separately as I build out the workshop.
